# JSP-000613 existing-proof evidence registration

## Catalog problem

**JSP-000613 / Erdős Problem 745**

The catalog asks how large the second-largest connected component is in the
critical transition window of a random graph.  At the reviewed awards revision
the entry is `Solved` but records `Lean proof: No`.

## Located public formalization

A complete public Lean development is available in `plby/lean-proofs`.

- Repository: https://github.com/plby/lean-proofs
- Pinned commit: `8822f7ddef30fadbd92e1c6ab4ed897af356af5e`
- Main source: `src/latest/ErdosProblems/Erdos745.lean`
- Critical theorem: `Erdos745.erdos_745`
- Supercritical theorem: `Erdos745.erdos_745_supercritical`
- Noncritical asymptotic theorem: `Erdos745.erdos745_noncritical_asymptotic`
- Noncritical scaling theorem: `Erdos745.erdos745_noncritical`
- Lean: `v4.33.0`
- Mathlib: `v4.33.0`

The source explicitly distinguishes the literal critical model
`G(n, 1/n)` from the supercritical model `G(n, λ/n)`, `λ > 1`.
For the literal critical parameter it proves that the second-largest component
has order `n^(2/3)` in probability.  The same development also proves the
correct logarithmic first-order law away from the critical point.

The ComparatorChallenges manifest selects all four theorems above and permits
only `propext`, `Classical.choice`, and `Quot.sound`.

## Mathematical attribution

The source itself identifies the supercritical logarithmic law with
Komlós--Sulyok--Szemerédi and identifies the critical `n^(2/3)` scaling with
Aldous's critical random-graph theorem.

Sprie claims neither mathematical-solution nor Lean-formalization authorship.
The contribution here is source discovery, statement correspondence, immutable
pinning and reproducible verification evidence.

The top-level pinned source does not state an individual formal author in its
header, so this evidence record does not invent one.  Any catalog display
credit should follow the awards repository's documented attribution convention
after verification.
